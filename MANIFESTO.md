# The ContentLens manifesto

The modern internet does not suffer from a lack of information. It suffers from an excess of irrelevant information.

Recommendation systems optimize for engagement. They reward outrage, repetition, manufactured urgency and content that is easy to consume. The user's goals are secondary.

ContentLens starts from a different premise: people should be able to optimize their feeds for knowledge, usefulness and personal intent.

## What we believe

### The feed should serve the user

A platform's recommendation engine is a source of candidates. It should not have the final word on what reaches the user.

### Personal relevance cannot be universal

There is no globally correct feed. A football video may be valuable to one person and noise to another. A user may reject devotional religious content while keeping academic discussion about religion. Rules need examples, exclusions and context.

### Explicit intent is the strongest signal

Watching a post does not mean approving it. A person may open something to investigate, criticize or verify it. ContentLens gives greater weight to explicit actions such as "always allow", "hide similar items" and "this classification is wrong".

### Known decisions should stay deterministic

If a user blocks a channel, author or exact term, the result should be immediate and absolute within the chosen scope. AI should not spend time reconsidering a decision the user has already made.

### AI decisions must be inspectable

The system should explain why it hid or reduced an item, show its confidence and allow a one-click correction. A personal curator must be auditable.

### Privacy is part of the architecture

Profiles, rules and feedback belong to the user. The product works locally without an account or a mandatory backend. Optional synchronization uses storage selected and owned by the user.

### The model should travel across platforms

Clickbait, rage bait, corporate fanfiction, AI filler and technical depth appear in many feeds. ContentLens models these as shared content archetypes while keeping DOM integration inside platform adapters.

## The goal

ContentLens aims to become a personal layer between people and algorithmic feeds: a lens that reduces noise, preserves useful exceptions and helps surface genuinely new information.

Take back control of your feeds.
